01Objective

Cardiovascular disease is one of the leading causes of death in India and worldwide, accounting for an estimated 28.1% of deaths. Early, reliable detection helps doctors start treatment in time. Using the given dataset, this project:

  • performs data pre-processing,
  • analyses and visualizes the data to find the main risk factors,
  • shows the correlation matrix of the features,
  • compares the accuracy of SVM, KNN, Decision Tree, Logistic Regression and Random Forest,
  • and builds a final model for heart disease detection based on the results.

03Data pre-processing

There were no missing values, but some records held values that are impossible for a living adult, such as a blood pressure of 16,020 mmHg, a height of 55 cm or a weight of 10 kg. Each suspicious group was listed and inspected before removal.

Two columns were added: age_years (age in days ÷ 365.25) and bmi (weight in kg ÷ height in m²).

  • High blood pressure is the strongest risk factor: 78.0% of patients with a reading of 140/90 or more have the disease, against 34.6% of those with normal blood pressure.
  • Cholesterol raises the rate from 43.5% (normal) to 76.2% (well above normal).
  • Glucose shows the same pattern, but weaker.
  • Gender makes almost no difference (49.2% of women, 50.0% of men). Active patients have slightly less disease. Smoking and alcohol show little difference; they are self-reported and few patients answered yes.

05Correlation matrix

Correlation runs from −1 to +1 and shows how strongly two features move together.

  • Systolic blood pressure has the strongest link with the disease (0.43), followed by diastolic pressure (0.34), age (0.24), cholesterol (0.22) and BMI (0.19).
  • Gender, height, smoking, alcohol and physical activity are almost unrelated to the disease on their own.
  • Some inputs overlap: systolic and diastolic pressure, weight and BMI, cholesterol and glucose, and gender and height.

06Accuracy of machine learning models

The 12 features (age, gender, height, weight, BMI, both blood pressures, cholesterol, glucose, smoking, alcohol, activity) were used to predict cardio. The data was split 80% for training (54,868 patients) and 20% for testing (13,717 patients), and scaled with StandardScaler so that features in different units count equally.

Random Forest gave the highest accuracy, 73.41%, so it was chosen as the final model. All five models are within 1 percentage point of each other. About 73% is the usual best result for this dataset, because important medical information such as family history and ECG results is not included.

07Final model: Random Forest

When the model says a patient has the disease, it is right 76% of the time (precision). It finds 68% of all patients who really have the disease (recall). Missed patients are the more serious error in medicine, so the model is best used as a screening aid alongside a doctor's diagnosis.

Systolic blood pressure accounts for 48.5% of the model's decisions, diastolic pressure for 20.4%, age for 12.6% and cholesterol for 8.4%. These are the same factors the analysis and the correlation matrix pointed to.

08Conclusion

  1. Pre-processing: no missing values. The id column, 24 duplicates and 1,391 records with impossible blood pressure, height, weight or BMI were removed, leaving 68,585 patients. Age was converted to years and BMI was added.
  2. Analysis: the classes are balanced. Heart disease rises sharply with age (23.4% → 67.1%), high blood pressure (34.6% → 78.0%) and cholesterol (43.5% → 76.2%); gender makes almost no difference.
  3. Correlation: systolic blood pressure (0.43) has the strongest link with the disease, followed by diastolic pressure, age, cholesterol and BMI.
  4. Model comparison: Random Forest 73.41%, SVM 73.27%, Decision Tree 73.16%, KNN 72.77%, Logistic Regression 72.65%.
  5. Final model: Random Forest, with the highest accuracy. It detects 68% of patients with the disease and is right 76% of the time when it flags one, so it is suited to quick screening that supports a doctor's diagnosis.

Possible improvements

  • Add medical features such as family history, ECG results and heart rate.
  • Tune the models further with GridSearchCV.
  • Lower the decision threshold to catch more patients with the disease (higher recall), at the cost of more false alarms.
